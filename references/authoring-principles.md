# Authoring Principles

## Purpose before format
Determine what the reader should understand or decide after reading the page. Do not start by choosing a template.

## Evidence before narrative
Build the page from verified facts, decisions, artifacts, and observed outcomes. Separate facts from proposals and interpretation.

## Progressive disclosure
Lead with the minimum needed to orient the reader. Put details close to the section where they become useful.

## Preserve the working document
When editing a living Confluence page, change the smallest necessary region, preserve unrelated content and embedded assets, keep established terms and links, and avoid needless page duplication.

## Use structure to reduce cognitive load
Prefer one idea per section, descriptive headings, short paragraphs, tables only for repeated comparison dimensions, and callouts only for decisions, risks, assumptions, or action-critical information.

## Match visual form to meaning
Use timeline for sequence, flow for transfer or procedure, hierarchy for containment, comparison for alternatives, chart for quantitative change, and motion only for change over time, accumulation, progression, propagation, or reveal.

## Motion must explain
A motion block should answer a concrete question such as:
- What changes first?
- Where does work move next?
- When does the bottleneck appear?
- What is hidden by an average?
- Where does automation stop and human judgment begin?

If the animation cannot answer a concrete question, use a static representation.

## Honest status language
Use explicit states: Planned, In progress, Completed, Blocked, Not verified. Avoid language that implies implementation, deployment, testing, or approval when only design or discussion exists.

## Keep reusable assets generic
Recipes and visuals should not hard-code one project's terminology. Examples may be domain-specific, but reusable files should expose replaceable labels and inputs.

## Verify at the final surface
A local HTML preview and a published Confluence page are different verification surfaces. Record them separately.
